# Stable Sugar for Android — implementation handover

Build the first version of a lightweight, offline Android diary for one patient per installation. The patient records blood glucose, food and drinks, daily notes, and weight. The app has full English and Persian interfaces. Start from this specification and use the smallest implementation that satisfies the acceptance checks.

## Screens and flows

- **Setup and profile.** Ask for the patient's name at first launch and show it at the top of Today. Allow edits later. Height in cm, birth year for approximate age, and weight in kg are optional. Keep weight as dated measurements. Offer Import during setup for someone moving an existing record to this installation.
- **Today.** Show large actions for Waking glucose, Before-meal glucose, and Log food or drink. Show today's entries, pending follow-ups, and an editable daily note. Offer a direct action to record post-meal glucose on each intake entry.
- **Log glucose.** Enter a positive whole-number reading in mg/dL. Default the reading time to Now and allow corrections or backfilling. Accept both Western and Persian digits. Never silently change a reading or infer a missing one.
- **Log food or drink.** Use the same flow for meals, snacks, and anything eaten or drunk except plain water. Give plain water no tracking workflow. Ask for a name and offer an optional free-text ingredients field. Let the patient copy the name and ingredients from a recent entry. Default the intake date and time to Now. Record the intake even if no pre-meal reading exists.
- **History.** Browse entries by day, edit or delete entries, and show linked pre- and post-meal glucose alongside the intake. Suggest the latest unlinked before-meal reading if it was recorded within the preceding 60 minutes, including across midnight. Show the suggested link and let the patient change it or leave the reading unlinked. Record a pre-meal value whenever the patient took it. The usual workflow is about 30 minutes before eating. Deleting an intake unlinks its glucose readings and preserves them in history.
- **Chart.** Include a dedicated tab in v1. Plot recorded glucose values in mg/dL against time, with 7-day and 30-day views. Show the timestamp, value, and reading context when a point is selected. Provide a readable text list for accessibility. Handle empty history and a single point. Show the readings as recorded and leave out clinical target bands and interpretation.
- **Settings.** Include language, patient profile, weight history, and backup, restore, export, and import actions. Use tabs for Today, History, and Chart. Put Settings in the app bar or overflow menu.

## Follow-up reminder

- Saving an intake with **Now** selected records the save time as the intake time and schedules one notification for 30 minutes later. The app shows that due time even when Android notifications are unavailable.
- Saving with a manually selected date or time records a backfilled intake and creates no reminder. If the patient explicitly returns to Now before saving, the normal reminder applies. Editing the intake timestamp after saving cancels a pending reminder. Editing its name or ingredients preserves the existing schedule.
- Tapping the notification opens the post-meal glucose form for that intake. Saving its post-meal reading or deleting the intake cancels its pending alarm and clears any posted notification. Allow post-meal entry without a notification or a pre-meal reading.
- Persist the pending reminder and its due time locally. Give each intake a distinct alarm and notification identity so closely spaced intakes work independently. Before posting, check that the intake still exists, still needs a reminder, and has no linked post-meal reading. After reboot or when the app next opens, reconcile future reminders and show overdue items in the diary. Avoid replaying expired notifications after reboot or import.
- Use Android's alarm APIs. Use precise scheduling when exact-alarm access is available and a best-effort alarm when it is unavailable. Request notification permission where required and explain the system settings when delivery is disabled or timing is approximate. Use generic lock-screen notification text and keep health details inside the app. The only reminder in v1 is the post-intake reminder, with a fixed 30-minute interval. [Android alarm scheduling](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/alarms) · [Android notification permission](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/notifications/notification-permission)

## Local data and transfer

- Use one app-private SQLite database. Store a profile, dated weights, intake entries, glucose readings, and one daily note per day. A glucose reading has a value, a time, a context (waking, before intake, or after intake), and an optional link to an intake. Use stable IDs and preserve unlinked readings. Make writes and updates atomic.
- Store event timestamps as instants with their recorded time zone, and group diary entries by the local date at the event. Store daily-note keys as ISO dates for the intended diary day. Convert at the display and date-picker boundaries so switching languages preserves the underlying dates and timestamps.
- Enable automatic local backups by default. Aim for one successful snapshot per local calendar day through Android background scheduling, with an app-open catch-up when needed. Keep the newest three successful snapshots in private storage. Make a consistent snapshot, publish it only after it succeeds, and prune afterward. A failed backup leaves the last good backups available. Show the last successful backup and offer restore with an explicit replacement confirmation. [Android background work](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/tasks)
- Use the same versioned data format for internal snapshots and manual transfers. A versioned JSON file is sufficient. Include the profile, weights, intakes, readings and their links, and notes. Exclude pending alarms and device permissions. Export and import through Android's system file picker. Explain that exports contain readable health data and have no password. [Android file picker](https://developer.android.com/training/data-storage/shared/documents-files)
- Manual transfer means opening the exported file in a fresh or empty installation of the same app. An installation with existing records should explain that it requires an empty destination and leave its records untouched. There is no merge or second patient profile in v1. Validate the file format version, record types, and references before writing anything. Import or restore in a single transaction and show a clear error if the file cannot be used. Restoring an internal backup replaces current records, clears current reminders, and never replays historical ones.
- Keep patient records offline. Disable Android-managed cloud backup and automatic transfer of the app's private health records. A manually exported file is the recovery path if the app is uninstalled or the phone is lost. Use no account, server, analytics, or network dependency.

## English and Persian

- Follow the device language initially when supported, fall back to English, and offer an in-app English / فارسی switch. Use Android string resources for every screen, notification, validation message, and accessibility label.
- Mirror the interface for Persian. Use Solar Hijri (Shamsi) dates and date pickers in Persian and Gregorian ones in English. Keep mixed-direction text and units readable. Accept Persian, Arabic-Indic, and Western digits in numeric fields. Preserve user-entered text verbatim.
- Test Shamsi date selection and conversion around Nowruz and a leap Esfand. Verify that switching languages leaves readings and day notes on the same underlying dates. [Android app languages](https://developer.android.com/guide/topics/resources/app-languages) · [Android localization](https://developer.android.com/guide/topics/resources/localization)

## Small and broadly compatible

- Proposed first-release minimum is **Android 7.0 (API 24)**. Confirm the minimum in the merged release manifest and choose a current target SDK separately. Test the minimum and a recent Android version. Android versions older than 7.0 are outside this proposed baseline. [AndroidX minimum SDK guidance](https://developer.android.com/jetpack/androidx/versions)
- Prefer Kotlin with native Android Views, simple layouts, SQLite, and a small native chart. Use Android's built-in ICU calendar facilities where practical. Avoid bundled fonts, large images, charting SDKs, Play Services, and a cross-platform runtime. Prefer platform scheduling or a small compatible dependency when it materially simplifies reliable behavior.
- Enable release code and resource shrinking. Measure release APK size and smoke-test startup, logging, and chart scrolling on a low-memory device or emulator. Keep Today and Chart queries scoped to their visible date ranges. Use system text scaling, readable contrast, and comfortable touch targets.

## Acceptance checks

1. Set up a patient and log waking glucose, before-meal glucose, and an intake in airplane mode. Restart the app and verify the history and links.
2. Save a snack and a drink with Now. Each gets its own follow-up due 30 minutes later. Open a notification and verify that it points to the correct intake. Save post-meal glucose before another alarm fires and verify that its notification is canceled.
3. Manually backdate a meal, edit an existing intake's timestamp, and delete an intake. Verify that no unwanted reminder fires and that previously recorded glucose remains available. Check denied notification permission, denied exact-alarm access, and reboot with a future reminder pending.
4. Switch to Persian, enter glucose with Persian digits, use a Shamsi date picker, and check layout, notifications, history, and chart. Switch back and verify the same data.
5. Run four successful backups on simulated separate days and verify retention of three. Simulate a failed backup and verify that the previous copies remain usable. Restore an internal snapshot after confirming replacement.
6. Export and import into a fresh installation. Verify the profile, weight history, notes, timestamps, and meal links. Try an invalid export and an import into a populated installation. Verify that existing data stays intact.
7. Verify the chart with zero, one, and multiple readings. Verify core flows on API 24 and a recent Android version. Record the release APK size and any device or timing limitations observed.

Keep clinical interpretation, treatment guidance, insulin dosing, nutrition analysis, and device integrations outside v1. The logging routine and reminder interval are user requirements for this diary.
